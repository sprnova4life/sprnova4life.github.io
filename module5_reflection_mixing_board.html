Track Notes
01

The experience that impacted me the most

Stephen Tonti's TEDx talk on ADHD stood out most clearly this module. He describes the space between knowing what to do and actually being able to start it. His description gave me a different account of behavior I had previously described as task avoidance.

What looked like resistance is often the space between intention and action. Building an accommodation around this idea changes what I do for the student in front of me.

02

How the history of special education still shapes the present

The earliest special education laws in this country guaranteed access to a room. They did not guarantee the quality of instruction happening inside it. This history still shows up as speed. A diagnosis often reaches a teacher before the student does, and it can settle into the whole account of who that student is before any actual teaching has happened.

A label carries information, and I use it. A label describes the access a student needs. It does not describe her capacity. Those are two separate facts, and I keep them separate on purpose.

03

High expectations for every student, including those with disabilities and those developing academic English

An IEP or a 504 plan describes the access a student needs to reach a standard. It does not lower the standard. Students who need heavier support and students who need more challenge often sit in the same room, and both groups need instruction built for where they actually are.

04

What "universal" actually means in UDL

Universal does not mean identical. It means a lesson gets built from the first draft to work across the actual range of students in the room, rather than built for the middle of the room with accommodations added afterward for whoever falls outside it.

05

Questions this module surfaced

Two questions are still open for me. The first is how to build in peer structure once group dynamics actually support it, since the safer default is always teacher-directed work. The second is where individualized pacing turns into isolation. Self-paced instruction is the entire design of this program, and it should not mean working alone.
